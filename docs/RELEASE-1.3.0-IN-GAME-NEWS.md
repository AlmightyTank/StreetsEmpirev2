Title:
StreetsEmpire 1.3: Businesses, Casinos & The Law

Body:
StreetsEmpire 1.3 is ready for main.

This is the first main release since 1.0.0, so this post covers the 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 work that has not been on main yet. The old core still matters: turns, street work, product, stores, raids, runs, turf, hideouts, alliances, jobs and season resets. What changed is the middle and late game.

Businesses, fronts and rackets

City Blocks are no longer only corners to hold. Every block has business lots tied to its district. Crews can build, staff, upgrade and collect from those businesses. Businesses keep earning over time, but registers cap, staff are committed while assigned, and built blocks are worth fighting over.

Rackets add choices. A business can stay quiet as a front or run stronger effects like laundering, VIP rooms, cheaper recon, better market hooks, more run capacity, defense help or extra cash with extra Heat. Player-owned built blocks now use block wars instead of instant flips, with warning, fights, siege progress, ally calls, take or sack goals, truce windows and war fatigue.

Casinos and gambling

Casinos are now real destinations. Las Vegas is the full casino town, but every city has a room with its own identity. Chips stay in their city, bosses have to be there to use the cage or tables, and games are server-run and saved against reconnects or retry double-spends.

Slots, Blackjack, Roulette, Street Dice and Poker are live. Rated play earns VIP status and comps. VIP status opens higher-limit rooms and raises bankroll ceilings, but it never changes odds. Comps buy hotel time only. Casino Jobs pay standing and cosmetics only, never money, product, turns or odds.

The law

Heat still rises fast, cools fast, reduces take and can trigger busts or arrests. 1.3 adds something slower: each city's police can now build a private Case on you.

A Case grows from part of the Heat and evidence you create in that city: busts, arrests, road stops, torching or sacking businesses, hijacks, big cash movement and racket noise. Other players cannot see your Case, add evidence to it or use it as a weapon.

Cases move through a wanted ladder: Quiet, Noticed, Under Investigation, Warrant and Federal. Under Investigation is a warning. Warrants have a response window and can target your Hideout, a business or you personally. Lawyers, officials and informants now give you ways to manage risk, but reckless play will still catch up.

Quality of life

New player navigation now focuses the early game on Dashboard, Scout, Stores, Produce, Raids and Jobs first. Advanced systems are still available, but tucked away until the getting-started guide is complete.

Businesses, casinos and law all have player-facing rules and help. Staff also get better operation views, Case receipt checks, audited corrections and stronger release-gate coverage. More profile titles, frames, accents and season feats were added, and the major new pages were checked across phone and desktop layouts.

What did not change

The 1.0 core loop is still the heart of the game. Gambling is not meant to be the best way to earn money. Businesses are not passive income without staff, supply and collection. The law does not replace Heat, busts, arrests or bribes. No player can add evidence to another player's Case. No Job, title, feat, frame or paid feature changes odds, combat math or Case math.

Start with the street loop and grow into the new systems. Build a block if you want territory to matter. Walk into a casino if you want to risk your own cash. Watch the Case panel if you keep a city hot.

See you on the streets.
