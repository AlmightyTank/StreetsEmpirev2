# StreetsEmpire Story Quest Campaign — 25 Main, 15 Side

**Status:** Original narrative proposal for the Tutorial & Story Quest Overhaul. These are new story treatments, not a claim that the quests are live. The campaign is written for StreetsEmpire's existing contacts and world; Escape from Tarkov is a reference for layered pacing and connected quest progression only.

## What the campaign is

This is a crime drama about favors that become debts, and debts that become a kind of ownership. The player begins as a small crew doing one ordinary job. A missing line on a receipt leads through stolen shipments, altered routes, planted pressure, and a private debt market called **the Clearinghouse**. The mystery grows because every contact holds one piece and each has a reason to keep it.

The Clearinghouse began as a way to settle debts quietly between people who could not use banks or courts. Its broker, **Mara Venn**, tells herself that moving a debt is kinder than collecting it by force. But she has learned that whoever keeps the book can decide which crews eat, which roads close, and which names the police notice. She did not create every betrayal in the story; she made a market where betrayal paid.

The campaign's central question changes as the player learns more:

1. **What went missing?**
2. **Who changed the record?**
3. **Who benefits when everyone owes someone?**
4. **What will the player do with the book once they have it?**

The story is about building an empire while deciding what kind of obligations it will live on. The player is not given a fixed biography or forced to agree with any contact. They earn a place in the story through the jobs they accept and the truths they choose to carry.

## Main storyline — 25 quests

The main line is designed as five acts. Quests 1–5 establish the crew and the mystery. Quests 6–8 are three investigation leads and can be completed in any order; all three resolve into Quest 9. The remaining chapters build on that evidence. No main quest teaches a core mechanic, requires a PvP victory, or locks a specialty path.

### Act I — A Name in the Rain

| # | Quest | Contact | Story chapter |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Night Receipt** | Mama | Mama hires the new crew for a quiet collection at a shuttered corner shop. The envelope is sealed, the amount is exact, and one line on the receipt has been rubbed almost through the paper. Mama says the job is finished. She keeps the receipt. |
| 2 | **A Small Favor** | Mama | The shopkeeper, Mrs. Alvarez, is still there after closing, sitting in the dark with a coat on indoors. She says she paid the debt months ago. Mama's name is on the receipt, but Mrs. Alvarez remembers a different name taking the money. |
| 3 | **The Other Door** | Pip | Pip's delivery was meant to reach the shop on the same night. The back door was opened before the driver arrived; no lock was forced, no guard was bribed. Pip wants the goods found. The player notices that the route on his copy was changed in a different hand. |
| 4 | **Roll Call** | Mama | Eli, Mama's young runner, misses two check-ins. Mama calls it a bad habit until she finds his last message tucked into the crew ledger: “Don't let them close the account for me.” She asks the player to find him before Tommy does. |
| 5 | **The Missing Line** | Ledger | Ledger compares the shop's receipt with Pip's dispatch slip. Both copies have the same narrow blank where a name should be. It is not a printer's fault: somebody removed the line from the original and made every later copy look clean. The name was **Mara Venn**. |

### Act II — Three Ways to Lose a Load

The player follows three independent leads. Each contact gives a partial truth; none can explain the blank line alone.

| # | Quest | Contact | Story chapter |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | **Pip's Count** | Pip | Pip's books say the shipment was complete. His scale says it was short before it ever reached the street. He accuses his supplier, then recognizes the supplier's initials on a debt transfer he signed without reading. |
| 7 | **The North Road** | Wheels | Wheels rebuilds the route from old driver notes. The truck took a turn that was never on the dispatch map. He remembers approving it himself after a caller said the main road was watched. The caller knew the crew's name. |
| 8 | **Tommy's Serial** | Tommy | A weapons crate found near the shuttered shop carries a serial from Tommy's own inventory. Tommy insists it was sold clean. His old tally book shows the buyer paid with a debt that had already been marked settled. Someone used Tommy's trust as a receipt. |
| 9 | **Two Buyers, One Load** | Player-led | The three accounts point to one transfer: the same goods were promised to two buyers, then used to make both believe the other had stolen from them. The player can now see the shape of the trick, but not who ordered it. |
| 10 | **A Favor Already Paid** | Vic | Vic admits he once asked Mara to bury a police notice before it reached a crew he was protecting. He thought he had paid in cash. Mara entered the favor as an open debt. Vic wants the record destroyed before the name attached to it gets hurt. |

### Act III — The Price of Keeping Quiet

| # | Quest | Contact | Story chapter |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | **Terms of Quiet** | Vic | Vic takes the player to the place where the notice was supposed to disappear. It is still there, folded behind a radiator, with a fresh date and an old signature. Vic's secret is not that he betrayed the crew; it is that he made a bargain he could not control. |
| 12 | **The Clerk on the Stairs** | Ledger | A retired bookkeeper named Mr. Hales recognizes Mara's mark. He says the Clearinghouse used to erase debts when both sides agreed. Then people began paying to move debts without telling the debtor. Hales kept one carbon copy because his daughter was on it. |
| 13 | **A Date Changed in Ink** | Tommy | Tommy compares a crew roster with a raid report. The report was prepared before the night the rival crew supposedly acted. The rival was paid to appear at the right time, but the report's author expected no one to be hurt. Someone turned a staged warning into a real fight. |
| 14 | **The Woman at Closing** | Mama | Mrs. Alvarez returns the second receipt. She had hidden it because the erased line was her husband's name. He did not run with the money; he signed away their shop to cover a debt he thought was already paid. Mama knew they were in trouble, but not who owned the paper. |
| 15 | **Your Name in the Margin** | Ledger | The player finds the crew's name in the newest book. It is not a debt the player remembers taking. It is a value assigned to future favors: routes, muscle, and silence. The Clearinghouse has started collecting before the crew agreed to owe anything. |

### Act IV — The Road Keeps Its Own Books

| # | Quest | Contact | Story chapter |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | **Mile Marker Nine** | Wheels | Wheels follows the altered route to a depot that no longer appears on city maps. The drivers there were not hiding stolen product. They were moving sealed account books between people who never met. He finds Eli's name on the passenger list. |
| 17 | **Passenger No. 9** | Player-led | Eli is alive, working under a borrowed name at the depot. He admits he carried one book because Mara promised to clear his mother's debt. He did not know it contained other people's names. He kept a torn page when he realized what it was. |
| 18 | **What Mama Bought** | Mama | The torn page shows Mama once accepted emergency money after a fire left several families without a place to sleep. She thought the repayment ended years ago. | 19 | **The Honest Scale** | Pip | Pip finds the account entry for the original shipment. The shortage was real, but the missing goods were used to cover a debt belonging to someone else. He can repair the supplier relationship, though doing so means admitting that his own books helped hide the transfer. |
| 20 | **The Map in the Margins** | Blocks | Blocks lays the torn page over a turf map. The marks follow a line of businesses and blocks, not a drug route. The Clearinghouse has been buying old debts attached to the corridor so that, when owners fall behind, the same buyer can take the whole strip. |

### Act V — Who Keeps the Book

| # | Quest | Contact | Story chapter |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 21 | **The Man Who Signed Twice** | Blocks | The last property record belongs to a local rival who signed two different settlement papers. He is not Mara's partner; he sold one favor to keep his crew alive and a second to keep his family out of the street. The player learns the network survives by making desperate choices look like treason. |
| 22 | **No One Owes Alone** | Player-led | The contacts compare what they know in one room for the first time. Their stories contradict each other, then fit: Mara did not need to command every betrayal. She only had to make sure each person believed they were the only one who had compromised. |
| 23 | **The Last Appointment** | Mara Venn | Mara asks the player to meet without weapons or witnesses. She offers to strike the crew's name from the book. In exchange, the player must leave the other names where they are. She believes a controlled debt market prevents open war. She is not lying about the violence it prevented; she is lying about who gets to control it. |
| 24 | **Everybody at the Table** | All contacts | The crew prepares for the final settlement. Each contact asks for something different: Mama wants the block left standing, Pip wants the supplier's name cleared, Tommy wants the staged raid acknowledged, Wheels wants the drivers left out of it, Vic wants the notice buried, and Blocks wants the corridor kept whole. No single answer gives everyone what they want. |
| 25 | **The Missing Line** | Player-led | At the shuttered shop, the player faces the original book and the people whose names were erased from it. They choose what the crew will do with the record: expose it, destroy it, or keep it under their own control. The choice closes the debt story, changes the contacts' final words, and sets the tone for the next campaign. |

## Side stories — 15 quests

These are five three-quest arcs. Each begins at a different main-story lead, follows a character who would otherwise disappear when the central mystery moves on, and closes a loose end in the campaign. Side stories add context and change who appears or speaks in the finale; they are not chores required to unlock the main ending.

| # | Quest | Branches from | Story and loose end resolved |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | **A Key Under the Mat** | Main 4 | Eli left a key with Mrs. Alvarez, not with a rival. She kept it because he once promised to return for a coat he left in her shop. The key opens a locker containing his first copied receipt. |
| S2 | **The Rent Envelope** | S1 | The shop's rent was paid in small envelopes after the listed owner disappeared. Mama remembers delivering one herself and realizes she was carrying money whose source she never checked. Mrs. Alvarez is given a chance to decide whether her name belongs in the final record. |
| S3 | **A Warm Room** | Main 18 | Mama and Eli return to the families helped after the fire. They find that the Clearinghouse used the relief money as proof that Mama had accepted a larger loan. Mama makes the repayment public, ending the lie even though it costs her pride. |
| S4 | **The Weight on the Scale** | Main 6 | Pip returns to the supplier and discovers two scales: one for customers and one for the books. The operator did not steal the load; he hid a shortage because his own family was being charged for it. |
| S5 | **A Box with No Label** | S4 | The operator shows Pip a crate he kept out of the count. Inside is ordinary stock with a debt code burned into the wood. Pip recognizes his handwriting on the old order and realizes he passed the code along himself. |
| S6 | **The Last Honest Count** | S5 | Pip corrects the supplier's ledger in front of the people it harmed. They do not forgive him on the spot. They agree to speak to the player because, for once, the books now match the truth. |
| S7 | **The Other Serial** | Main 8 | Tommy tracks a second weapon serial to an apprentice he fired years ago. The apprentice did not sell the crate; Tommy's old partner sold the same lot twice and blamed the youngest person on the rack. |
| S8 | **Last Shift at the Armory** | S7 | The apprentice still has the original sale book, but wants Tommy to admit why he stopped asking questions. Tommy's answer is that he needed the shop open more than he needed to be right. The book supplies a witness for the staged-raid lead. |
| S9 | **A Rack Kept Open** | S8 | Tommy offers the apprentice their job back; they refuse the old terms and ask to run the count themselves. Tommy agrees. The weapon trail is closed, and Tommy learns to trust a record he did not write. |
| S10 | **Mile Marker Zero** | Main 7 | Wheels returns to the first route he ever taught Eli. A retired driver confirms the road was changed by a voice using Wheels' own call sign. He cannot remember the face, but remembers the driver waiting to be paid. |
| S11 | **The Passenger Window** | Main 17 | Eli describes the other people on the depot run: one driver who knew, one who only needed money, and one who thought the trip was a normal delivery. Wheels refuses to let the player label them all informants. |
| S12 | **Every Road Home** | S11 | Wheels meets the drivers at a neutral stop and offers them a route out of the work. One accepts, one refuses, and one leaves without answering. The player gets the route ledger without pretending every loose end can be tied neatly. |
| S13 | **A Copy for Ledger** | Main 12 | Hales's daughter has the missing carbon copy but will not hand it to another fixer. Ledger must explain what the evidence can and cannot prove before she agrees to let him compare it. |
| S14 | **The Holding Company** | Main 20 | Blocks follows the corridor's ownership through three shell businesses. The papers are legal; the pressure used to acquire the debts is not. He learns which local rival sold a signature and why. |
| S15 | **Terms in Plain English** | S13 and S14 | Vic, Ledger, and Blocks sit with the rival and read every page aloud. The rival gives a truthful statement, not an apology. This closes the property and legal-record threads before the final settlement. |

## Branches and endings

- **Investigation order:** Main quests 6, 7, and 8 can be completed in any order. Each reveals a different mechanism—false inventory, altered routes, or reused weapons records—and all three lead to Quest 9.
- **Side-story order:** Each side arc unlocks from its listed main quest. Its three chapters can be completed independently of the other side arcs.
- **Ending choice:** Quest 25 offers three narrative outcomes: **Open the Book** (expose the records and accept the fallout), **Burn the Book** (break the debt chain but lose proof), or **Keep the Book** (take control of the ledger and risk becoming its next keeper). Each closes the central conflict and changes later contact dialogue; none grants a stronger gameplay reward or blocks a specialty.
- **Loose ends:** Side arcs resolve the human cost behind the clues: Eli's disappearance, Mama's unspoken debt, Pip's false count, Tommy's scapegoated apprentice, Wheels' drivers, and the corridor's coerced signatures.
- **Persistent history:** Store branch and ending choices with stable server-side keys. The story archive shows the player's actual choice and its consequence; it never invents a branch result from current stats.

## Prose standard

Each quest should read like a short chapter in a crime novel: a distinct opening image, a character who wants something specific, a complication that changes the reader's understanding, and a closing beat that pulls the next chapter forward. Quest objectives can be concise, but narrative text should not be reduced to a one-line flavor quote. The tutorial alone explains how systems work.

### Opening scene sample — “The Night Receipt”

Rain worked the avenue into a blur of red signs and black windows. Mama had left the porch light off. She slid an envelope across the table with two fingers, careful not to touch the damp corner.

“Mrs. Alvarez paid on Friday,” she said. “Tonight you take her receipt back. No speech, no collection. Just make sure the paper gets home.”

The amount was right. The date was right. On the last line, somebody had pressed a pen hard enough to score the paper, then rubbed the name away. Mama watched the player read it.

“You know whose name that was?”

For a moment, the rain filled the room.

“I know who wants us asking,” Mama said. “That’s why you’re taking it back.”

## Tutorial boundary and production rule

The tutorial remains the only system that teaches mechanics and gates feature access. The campaign assumes those lessons are available, but story quests never require a tutorial reward, skip, or choice. A main-story objective may use normal game actions; its narrative purpose is always clear from the contact's request.

Before implementation, map all 40 story treatments to existing quest IDs, objective evidence, rewards, contact profiles, and round-pinned rulesets. Preserve completed progress and never replay historical rewards. Ship the first complete contact arc as a pilot, then expand the same server-owned chapter/choice model to the full campaign.
